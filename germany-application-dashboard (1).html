The language reality

The programme is advertised as English-taught and the regulations say instruction is "usually English." But the German-only modules are precisely the electrical engineering ones — microelectronics, sensors, control theory, drives, electromagnetics, assembly technology. The English modules are the computer science ones.

Without German this becomes a CS-heavy embedded degree in practice. It is entirely completable: roughly 158 credits are reachable in English against the 61–69 you need from lectures and seminars. But the hardware half is closed until your German is strong enough.

Two silver linings. Mikroelektronik 2–4 are the VLSI-adjacent modules you didn't want anyway. And the handbook shows minor ECTS inconsistencies between module pages and Appendix A — Messtechnik lists 4 on its page and 6 in the appendix — so confirm credit values before planning a semester tightly.

What a summer start actually costs

Both module tables carry the note "Semester = start in winter semester assumed," and the example study plan is built for a winter start. Semester 4 is the thesis, so coursework runs across semesters 1–3: Su 2027 → Wi 2027/28 → Su 2028.

Requirements are not at risk. Your first semester offers Foundations of AI, Image Processing, and Software Engineering — all three CS essentials you need, 18 ECTS — plus Signal Processing and Assembly and Packaging, 12 more. A full 30-credit semester that clears one whole area.

The real cost is risk concentration. Twelve of the seventeen essential modules are winter-only, so with a summer start they all land in semester 2 with no second window. Specifically: Sensors and Modelling and System Identification — the two courses that let you satisfy Advanced MSE without touching microfabrication — are both winter-only. If either falls through, what remains in that table is exactly the material you wanted to avoid. Sensors is lab-based and capacity-limited, which makes it the single fragile point. Failure isn't fatal (auto-registration for retakes, Studienleistungen repeatable indefinitely, a fifth semester as fallback); missing a seat is the sharper risk.

Worth remembering

  • PhD is a stated qualification goalGraduates are described in the handbook as "capable of further qualification through a doctorate program (PhD)". Medical engineering is named among the main application fields.
  • External theses are permitted but procedurally strictThe examiner signs a confirmation of supervision, issued only after you submit an approved individual study plan. That plan cannot be changed later, and working on the thesis before formal approval is not permitted. Plan the Werkstudent-to-thesis route early rather than improvising it.
  • You may be able to transfer credits inRecognition of external achievements is possible — including from outside the higher education system, at institutions with standardised quality assurance. Refused if more than 50% of the curriculum would be substituted. The application must be made within your first semester after enrolment. With three years of professional engineering behind you, worth exploring.
  • Exactly one lab course inside your fieldPlus at most one more as an elective. Labs run 6–9 CP.
  • Eight semesters maximumAgainst four standard. More headroom than most.
  • Double degrees availableGrenoble INP, Sofia, Budapest — binational study without losing time. Plus Erasmus+, free-mover semesters, and Eucor (the trinational Upper Rhine campus) for individual courses or full semesters.
  • The exemplary study plan is staleIt's labelled SPO 2023 while the rest of the handbook is SPO 2025, and it assumes a winter start. Treat as illustrative. As with Freiburg, check the winter/summer column against each module — a summer start reorders availability.
  • Best of both rankingsTU9 and Excellence University, in the strongest job region of anywhere you're applying — Karlsruhe's IT cluster, with Stuttgart's automotive belt an hour away. If the GRE retake succeeds, this is the best outcome available to you.
